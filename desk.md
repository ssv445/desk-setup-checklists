# Desk checklist, sitting or standing

You order the desk that looks good in the photos. On day one your knees knock the drawer, and the screen sits so close you push your chair back to read it.

This list has three parts: measure at home, check in the shop or on the product page, and walk away if you see a fault on the last list.

**Your numbers:** your sitting and standing desk heights, and your seat height, come from your height. For numbers worked out from your height, use the online [standing desk buying guide](https://poxture.com/guides/how-to-choose-a-desk). It covers sitting desks too.

## 1. Measure at home before you go

1. **Your height → your desk height, sitting and standing.**
   How: stand barefoot against a wall, mark the top of your head, measure floor to mark. If you work in shoes, note that too. Every height goes up a little. Each desk height is the one that puts your keyboard level with your elbows. [1, 4]
2. **Your reach → the depth you need.**
   How: sit back in a chair at a table, keyboard where you type. Raise one arm straight ahead. Your fingertips mark where the screen goes. Measure from the table's front edge to your fingertips. Write it down as your reach. [6]
3. **Your keyboard, mouse and screen → the width you need.**
   How: lay out your keyboard and mouse side by side as you use them, and measure across. Measure across your screen too (two screens: both). Take the wider number and add room for what you use every day, like a laptop or a paper notebook. Write it down as your width. [4]
4. **Your thigh height → the leg room you need.**
   How: sit on a chair set to your seat height, feet flat. Measure from the floor to the highest point on top of your thigh. Write it down as your thigh height.
5. **Your room → the space you have.**
   How: measure the wall where the desk goes and the floor in front of it, with room behind your chair to push it back. [8]

## 2. Check in the shop or on the product page

Each line is a measurement or a test that takes about 30 seconds.

- [ ] **A fixed desk: the top sits within your sitting desk height.**
  Check: measure from the floor to the top ("height" in the product's sizes). [1, 4]
  - Higher than that: you'll raise your chair to meet it and put a box or a step stool under your feet. The [office chair checklist](office-chair.md) covers the seat height for that desk. [2]
  - Lower than that: furniture risers, sturdy blocks made to lift a desk, can raise it. [2]
- [ ] **It's deep enough for your screen at arm's length.**
  Check: the depth is at least your reach plus the monitor's "depth with stand" (on the monitor's product page: the monitor and its stand measured front to back). A corner desk gives more depth. Then check the screen itself with the [monitor checklist](monitor.md). [2, 4]
- [ ] **It's wide enough.**
  Check: the width is at least your width number. [4]
- [ ] **It fits your room.**
  Check: its width and depth fit the space you measured, with room behind your chair to push it back. [8]
- [ ] **Your legs fit underneath.**
  Check: the open space under the top is at least 52 cm (20 in) wide, 44 cm (17 in) deep at your knees and 60 cm (24 in) deep at your feet. Measure between the desk's legs, side panels or drawers. [3]
- [ ] **Nothing under the top touches your thighs.**
  Check: at your sitting desk height, the lowest thing under the top (a drawer, a crossbar or the frame) sits above your thigh height, with room to move your legs. In the shop, sit on a chair at your seat height and roll right in. [2]
- [ ] **It holds still when you lean on it.**
  Check: lean on the front edge and type hard. The top doesn't sway, shake or rock. [6]
- [ ] **The front edge is rounded.**
  Check: rest your forearms on the edge. It's round or sloped, not a sharp corner. [2, 4]
- [ ] **The top doesn't shine.**
  Check: look for the ceiling lights in the top. A matte finish, not glass. [4]

### Standing desk: three more checks

- [ ] **Its height range covers both your numbers.**
  Check: lowest setting at or below the low end of your sitting desk height, highest at or above the high end of your standing desk height ("height range" in the product's sizes). [4, 5]
- [ ] **It holds still at your standing desk height.**
  Check: raise it to your standing desk height. Lean on the front edge and type hard. The top doesn't sway or shake. [6]
- [ ] **It moves smoothly.**
  Check: run it all the way down and up. It moves without jerking, and your cables reach at both ends. [4, 5]

One note before you pay: check the return window. You can't feel wobble on a product page, and leg room is easy to misjudge from numbers, so plan to sit and stand at it at home in the first days.

## 3. Walk away if you see this

1. **A standing desk that can't go down to the low end of your sitting desk height, or up to the high end of your standing desk height.** [5]
2. **Leg room narrower than 52 cm (20 in), or shallower than 44 cm (17 in) at your knees.** [3]
3. **A drawer or crossbar that touches your thighs** at your sitting desk height and can't come off. [2]
4. **The top wobbles** when you lean on it, at any height you'll use. [6]
5. **It's too shallow for your reach plus the monitor's depth with stand**, and you won't add a monitor arm. You'll end up sitting too close to the screen. [2, 4]
6. **A glass top.** [4]
