# Office chair checklist

You buy the chair with the best reviews. By Friday the seat edge presses behind your knees, and you're perched on the front half of it to reach your keyboard.

This list has three parts: measure at home, check in the shop or on the product page, and walk away if you see a fault on the last list.

**Your numbers:** your seat height comes from your height. For numbers worked out from your height, use the guide on [how to choose an office chair](https://poxture.com/guides/how-to-choose-an-office-chair).

## 1. Measure at home before you go

1. **Your height → your seat height.**
   How: stand barefoot against a wall, mark the top of your head, measure floor to mark. Note it if you work in shoes. [1]
2. **Your thigh length → the seat depth you need.**
   How: sit on a hard chair with your back against the backrest. Measure from the backrest to the back of your knee. Write it down as your thigh number.
3. **Your hip width → the seat width you need.**
   How: sit on a hard, flat seat and measure straight across the widest part of your hips and thighs.
4. **Your desk height, if the desk stays.**
   How: measure from the floor to the top of the desk. Your chair has to reach the seat height that puts your elbows level with that desk.
   - If your desk is more than 2 cm (1 in) higher than suits your seat height, your feet won't reach the floor there. Plan a footrest: a box or a step stool.
   - If it's more than 2 cm (1 in) lower, the desk is low for you. Furniture risers, sturdy blocks made to lift a desk, can raise it.
5. **Your floor: carpet or hard.**
   How: look down. You'll match the chair's wheels to it.

## 2. Check in the shop or on the product page

Set the chair to your seat height first, then sit back fully. Each line takes about 30 seconds.

- [ ] **The seat height range covers your seat height.**
  Check: lowest setting at or below the low end, highest at or above the high end ("seat height" in the product's sizes). If not, ask for a shorter or taller gas lift, the cylinder under the seat. [1, 3, 6]
- [ ] **Your feet rest flat with your thighs level.**
  Check: at your seat height, sit with your feet flat. The front of your knees is level with your hips or slightly lower, and nothing presses under your thighs. [3, 4]
- [ ] **If your desk stays, the seat also reaches the seat height for that desk.**
  Check: the highest setting is at or above it. [2]
- [ ] **A fist fits behind your knees.**
  Check: back against the backrest, your closed fist, about 5 cm (2 in), fits between the seat edge and your calves. Online: seat depth at least 5 cm (2 in) under your thigh number, or a sliding seat. [2, 5]
- [ ] **The seat is wider than your hips.**
  Check: at least 2.5 cm (1 in) of seat shows on each side of your hips and thighs. On a product page: seat width is your hip width plus 5 cm (2 in) or more. [4]
- [ ] **The seat's front edge curves down.**
  Check: run your hand over the front edge. It rounds toward the floor and doesn't dig in behind your knees. [2, 4]
- [ ] **The lower-back support meets the hollow of your lower back.**
  Check: sit back. The backrest's bulge sits in the hollow of your lower back. If it doesn't, move it up or down. [2, 5]
- [ ] **Your hips fit against the backrest.**
  Check: sit right back. You don't get pushed forward onto the front of the seat. [4]
- [ ] **The backrest leans back with you.**
  Check: lean back. It reclines and still supports your back. [4, 5]
- [ ] **The armrests go low enough.**
  Check: arms by your sides, elbows bent to about 90°. The armrests can sit just under your elbows. At their lowest, they don't lift your elbows. [2]
- [ ] **The armrests let you reach the desk.**
  Check: roll up to a desk or table; your forearms reach the keyboard spot without leaning forward. Armrests 25 cm (10 in) or shorter, or ones that flip up or come off, are a good sign. [2, 5]
- [ ] **The armrests move in and out.**
  Check: slide them in and out. Your upper arms stay close to your body with your forearms resting. [4, 5]
- [ ] **You can work every lever while sitting.**
  Check: change the seat height without standing up. [3, 4]
- [ ] **The base has five legs.**
  Check: count the legs. [3, 5]
- [ ] **The wheels suit your floor.**
  Check: hard wheels for carpet, soft wheels for a hard floor. On a product page, look for "carpet" or "hard floor" in the wheel details. [3]
- [ ] **The seat holds its height.**
  Check: sit down firmly a few times. The seat doesn't sink, and nothing wobbles or rattles. [6]

One note before you pay: check the return window. Cornell says to see if the seat still feels right after 60 to 120 minutes of sitting, and a shop visit is too short for that. [4]

## 3. Walk away if you see this

1. **At its lowest, your feet still don't rest flat**, and no shorter lift is offered.
2. **At its highest, the seat stays below the high end of your seat height**, or below the seat height for your desk if it stays, and no taller lift is offered.
3. **The seat edge touches the back of your knees** when your back is against the backrest, and the seat doesn't slide. It catches you behind the knees or keeps your back off the backrest, so you may end up sliding down your chair. [4]
4. **The armrests keep you away from the desk** and can't go lower, flip up or come off. You'll lean forward to type and end up hunched over your desk.
5. **The armrests, at their lowest, lift your elbows**, and they can't flip up or come off. [2]
6. **Four legs or fewer on wheels.** OSHA says these may tip. [5]
7. **The seat sinks when you sit, or the chair wobbles.** [6]
8. **The lower-back support misses the hollow of your back** and doesn't move.
9. **You have to stand up to change the height.** [3]
